# 🌱 Aahar AI Community Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

Aahar AI welcomes people who want to explore food technology, AI-assisted experiences, frontend engineering, and open collaboration. This Code of Conduct defines the expectations for anyone participating in the repository and project-managed spaces.

We want collaboration to be respectful, inclusive, practical, and focused on improving the project. Technical disagreement is welcome. Harassment, intimidation, humiliation, and discriminatory behaviour are not.

By participating in this repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, or project-related communication, you agree to follow this policy.

## 🧭 Scope

This policy applies to project spaces such as:
- GitHub issues and comments.
- Pull requests and code reviews.
- Discussions where enabled.
- Repository documentation and contribution channels.
- Project-related online events where participants represent or collaborate on Aahar AI.

It may also apply to conduct outside these spaces when it is directly connected to project participation and materially affects another participant's ability to contribute safely.

## ✅ Expected Behaviour

Participants are expected to:

### Communicate respectfully
- Use professional and considerate language.
- Critique ideas, code, and decisions rather than attacking people.
- Avoid assumptions about another person's ability or intent.
- Respect different experience levels and communication styles.
- Keep discussion relevant and allow reasonable time for responses.

### Give useful technical feedback
- Explain the problem and practical impact.
- Include reproduction steps or evidence where possible.
- Distinguish a defect from a personal preference.
- Suggest alternatives where practical.
- Acknowledge when a concern has been resolved.

### Support inclusive participation
- Welcome beginner questions without ridicule.
- Avoid discriminatory language and stereotypes.
- Respect names, pronouns, and personal boundaries.
- Avoid unnecessary gatekeeping.
- Make a reasonable effort to keep examples and documentation accessible.

### Be honest and responsible
- Represent contributions and test results accurately.
- Credit work appropriately.
- Do not fabricate benchmarks, approvals, or test output.
- Do not imply that an open proposal is already shipped.
- Respect privacy and confidential information.

## 🚫 Unacceptable Behaviour

Unacceptable conduct includes:
- Harassment, discrimination, threats, intimidation, or targeted personal attacks.
- Hate speech or demeaning language.
- Sexual harassment or unwanted sexualized attention.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Stalking, doxxing, or repeated unwanted contact.
- Trolling or sustained bad-faith disruption.
- Impersonation or deceptive attribution.
- Threats of violence or encouragement of harm.
- Retaliation against someone reporting a concern in good faith.
- Deliberately exposing credentials, private messages, or visitor information.
- Repeatedly ignoring reasonable moderation instructions.
- Flooding project spaces with automated or irrelevant content.

A single severe incident may warrant immediate action. Repeated lower-level incidents may also become unacceptable as a pattern.

## 💬 Technical Disagreement

Strong technical disagreement is allowed. Participants may challenge model choices, prompt design, API architecture, state management, dependencies, UI design, data handling, and roadmap priorities.

A productive review:
1. Identifies the relevant code or decision.
2. Explains the concern and impact.
3. Provides evidence where possible.
4. Separates correctness from preference.
5. Offers a reasoned alternative when known.
6. Avoids personal remarks.

Maintainers may decline contributions that are unsafe, out of scope, incomplete, or inconsistent with project direction. A respectful technical rejection is not misconduct.

## 📣 Reporting a Concern

Contact the repository owner privately through the contact method listed on the owner's GitHub profile.

When reporting:
- Do not start a public argument.
- Share only information necessary to explain the concern.
- Include links and approximate timestamps where available.
- Explain whether there is an immediate safety or privacy concern.
- Avoid publishing private messages or identifying details.

For security vulnerabilities, use the separate process in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## 📝 Information for Reports

A report may include:
- The project space where the incident occurred.
- A link to the relevant issue, PR, comment, or discussion.
- A concise description of the behaviour.
- Approximate date and time.
- Relevant context and impact.
- Whether the behaviour has happened previously.
- Any immediate safety concern.
- What support or outcome would help, if known.

You do not need to investigate the incident yourself or confront the other person before reporting it.

## 🛠️ Maintainer Responsibilities

Maintainers should:
- Review concerns seriously and as impartially as possible.
- Protect privacy where practical.
- Limit report details to people who need them.
- Avoid unnecessary public disclosure.
- Consider the impact on all involved.
- Apply this policy consistently and proportionately.
- Explain decisions when doing so does not compromise privacy or safety.
- Avoid being the sole decision-maker when directly involved in a report, where practical.

## ⚖️ Enforcement

Depending on severity and context, maintainers may:
1. Request a correction or change in behaviour.
2. Issue a formal warning.
3. Remove or moderate harmful content.
4. Temporarily restrict participation.
5. Permanently exclude someone from project spaces.

These actions are not a mandatory sequence. Severe threats, doxxing, or targeted harassment may require immediate restriction.

## 🔒 Confidentiality and Retaliation

Reports should be handled with discretion, but absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed because limited details may need to be shared during an investigation.

Retaliation against someone who reports a concern in good faith or supports an investigation is prohibited. A report should not be used to silence reasonable criticism or avoid technical review.

## 📨 Appeals

A person who believes an enforcement decision was based on materially incorrect information may contact the repository owner privately with a concise explanation and relevant evidence.

An appeal is not a guarantee that a decision will be reversed. Repeated appeals without new information may be declined.
